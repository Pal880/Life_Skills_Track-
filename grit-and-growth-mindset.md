# Grit and Growth Mindset: Answers

## Q1: Paraphrase (summarize) the video in a few (1 or 2) lines. Use your own words.

Duckworth explains that long-term success depends less on IQ or talent and more on **grit**, which is passion and perseverance for long-term goals. She suggests that building a growth mindset, believing ability can improve with effort, helps people develop grit.

## Q2: Paraphrase (summarize) the video in a few (1 or 2) lines in your own words.

Growth mindset, from Carol Dweck's research, is the belief that abilities can be developed through effort, good strategies, and feedback, unlike a fixed mindset, which treats them as unchangeable. People with a growth mindset embrace challenges and learn from mistakes, which helps them improve in every area of life.

## Q3: What is the Internal Locus of Control? What is the key point in the video?

**Internal locus of control** means believing that your results come mainly from your own actions, choices, and effort, not from luck or other people.

**Key point:** Focus on what you can control and take responsibility for it. This keeps you motivated, whereas blaming outside factors (external locus) makes you feel helpless.

## Q4:  What are the key points mentioned by speaker to build growth mindset (explanation not needed).

- Embrace challenges
- Learn from mistakes and failures
- Use the word "yet" ("I can't do this yet")
- Value effort and the learning process
- Welcome feedback and criticism
- Learn from others' success instead of feeling threatened
- Set learning goals, not just performance goals
- Be persistent when things get hard

## Q5: What are your ideas to take action and build Growth Mindset?

- Take **100% responsibility** for my learning
- Stay with a problem until I solve it, and not quit when it gets hard
- Never write code I don't understand
- Replace "I can't" with "I can't **yet**"
- Treat bugs and errors as chances to learn
- Ask for feedback and doubts early, without fear of looking less capable
- Practice daily and track my progress
- Celebrate effort and small improvements, not just results
