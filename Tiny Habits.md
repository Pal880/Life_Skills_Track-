
## Q1: In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

Fogg's idea of starting with a **tiny behavior** anchored to an existing routine, like doing 2 push-ups after using the bathroom. It grows naturally, because starting small removes the need for willpower.

## Q2: How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P.

**Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt** (all three must come together).

- **M (Motivation):** your desire to do it
- **A (Ability):** how easy it is to do
- **P (Prompt):** the cue that reminds you to do it

To make habits easier: shrink the habit (high ability), attach it to an existing routine as a prompt ("After I brush my teeth, I will floss one tooth"), and don't rely on motivation.

## Q3: Why it is important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit? (This is the most important concept in today's topic. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit)


Celebrating creates a **positive emotion right after the behavior**, and emotion is what wires the habit into your brain. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit, and it also builds confidence and motivation.

The story of **marginal gains**: the British cycling team improved many small things by 1% each and went on to win major titles. Tiny improvements compound over time.

## Q5: What is the book's perspective about Identity?

Lasting change comes from changing **who you believe you are**, not just what you want to achieve. Each action is a **vote for your identity** ("I am a person who exercises"), and habits are how you prove it to yourself.

## Q6: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?

The four laws:

- Make it **obvious** (clear cues, habit stacking)
- Make it **attractive** (pair it with something you enjoy)
- Make it **easy** (reduce friction, use the **2-minute rule**, prepare your environment)
- Make it **satisfying** (immediate reward, track progress)

## Q7: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

Invert the laws:

- Make it **invisible** (remove cues)
- Make it **unattractive** (focus on its downsides)
- Make it **difficult** (add friction, like deleting apps or logging out)
- Make it **unsatisfying** (accountability partner, habit contract or penalty)

## Q8: Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and or response satisfying?

- **Obvious:** after my morning tea, I open my laptop with the editor already open
- **Attractive:** listen to my favorite playlist while coding
- **Easy:** start with just 10 minutes or one problem
- **Satisfying:** tick off a habit tracker and celebrate ("Shine") after each session

## Q9: Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue invisible or the process unattractive or hard or the response unsatisfying?

- **Invisible:** keep the phone in another room, and turn off notifications
- **Unattractive:** remind myself how much time it wastes
- **Hard:** log out of apps, set app timers, and use grayscale mode
- **Unsatisfying:** tell a friend my screen-time goal so they can check on me
